《止音纪元》· 原创短篇全集 · 双语对照

The Epoch of Hush · The Complete Companion Fiction · Bilingual

钟不是用来敲的。钟是用来记住：某个声音曾经来过，某个沉默曾经抵达，某个没说出口的字，此刻还住在你身上。——这一册里的每一个故事，都是从沉默里捡回来的声音。它们不讲胜利，不讲救赎，不讲新神。它们讲的是——当世界把所有的话都还给收音机之后，人还能剩下什么。

A bell is not meant to be struck. It is meant to remember: that a voice once passed through, that a silence once arrived, that a word never spoken still lodges in you. Every story in this volume is a voice picked out of silence. No victory, no salvation, no new gods — only this: when the world has returned every sentence to the receiver, what is left of a person?

“The Fourth Strike”

《秤旁》

“Beside the Scale”

铜扣和铁钉并排放着。阿汐没有数它们——她不再数了。窗台的空处仍在，薄页上的空格也仍在。渡口栏杆上挂着一小截粉笔头。海经过门外，没有替它记下声音。

The button and the nail lay side by side. Axi did not count them — she no longer counts. The empty space on the sill remained; so did the blank in the thin ledger; the sea passed outside the door, leaving its sound unrecorded.

《未记的一页》

“The Unentered Page”

阿汐看了很久，却没有抄进薄簿里。夜里涨潮，横线被盐水盖住；天亮时，木板上只剩一小块干处。她把薄簿合上，没把那块干处算进去。海没有抹掉那条线——它只是替它换了件衣服。

Axi studied the faint line on the sill, but did not copy it into the ledger. At night the tide covered it with salt water. By morning, only a small dry patch remained on the wood; she closed the ledger without counting it. The sea did not wipe the line away — it only gave it a different cloth to wear.

No. 35 · The Arcade of Echoes · The Dry Place

《干处》

“The Dry Place”

潮水退去了，它还在那里；涨回来时，木板的其他地方都湿了，那一小块地方没有变湿。它比周围的木板大不了一枚指甲盖。盐水爬上木板，涨到它边缘，停在一指以外——就那么多。

The tide could not reach one small place on the plank — when the flood returned, the boards everywhere else became dark and wet, and that place did not. Salt water rose to within a finger's width of its edge and stopped there, no farther.

《未称之物》

“Unweighed”

干处旁边，铜扣、铁钉和薄簿都没有动。渡人把小铜秤放在门槛上——秤盘空着。他没有问阿汐要称什么。潮水退去时，秤盘里多了一粒盐。渡人也蹲下来看它——他在几寸外停住手指，没有碰它。

Beside the dry place, the button, nail, and thin ledger lay untouched. The ferryman set his small brass scale on the threshold — the pan empty. He did not ask Axi what it should weigh. At ebb, a grain of salt lay in the pan; the ferryman held his finger a handspan above it without touching it.

This volume ends here — but the aftersound does not.
“The Dark Tide,” the eleventh story, tells the Dark Forest law of cosmic sociology —
the twelfth, “Epilogue · The Twelfth Listener,” is written for the one who turned here —
the thirteenth, “The Bell-Picker,” gathers what each bell left behind — for the one who rings the fourth —
the fourteenth, “The Lampwright,” is proof that broken copper still remembers —
the fifteenth, “The City of Low Tones” — a debt paid in listening clears without a coin —
the sixteenth, “The Pendulum Ferryman” — completing what was never finished is the completion itself —
the seventeenth, “The Unfinished Ledger Page” — the blank in the final line waits to be claimed —
the eighteenth, “The Fourth Strike” — the fourth never needed a bell; it always lands in your own chest —
the nineteenth, “The Last String” — the world was loud; then a string on the north cliff found itself —
the twentieth, “The String-Listener” — the string asked the world a question, and finally someone answered —
the twenty-first, “The Third Voice” — the night the lamp went out, the sea said: I hear too —
the twenty-second, “The Ensemble” — four people, one second, the sea said and the string and the pen and the table all held still —
the twenty-third, “The Echo Vale” — every sound the world had forgotten was gathered here, held, and kept —
the twenty-fourth, “After Half a Chime” — a trigger of a bell that was never rung walks itself into the Arcade —
the twenty-fifth, “Beside the Button” — the chalk line stays; the button stops beyond it; Axi presses —
the twenty-sixth, “Beyond the Line” — the bag she could not hold was blown away; what came back was one line she never wrote —
the twenty-seventh, “After the Bag” — the mouth stays folded over the line, she adds no word; one night the door opens a finger’s width —
the twenty-eighth, “Salt on the Sill” — the salt line breaks clean at the boards’ seam on the fifth night; she lifts the corner and lets the wind in and out —
the twenty-ninth, “After the Salt Line” — the door stays shut; the bag by the door; the two halves on the sill growing back ever so slightly —
the thirtieth, “The Sill Keeps Count” — the bag stays; the halves side by side; the empty space between them; even the ebb keeps count —
the thirty-first, “After the Space” — the copper button grows on the sill; she sets it by the floorboard seam; the boards never learn her count —
the thirty-second, “The Unmarked Number” — the ferryman weighs the button; the needle never shifts; the ledger keeps a blank —
the thirty-third, “Beside the Scale” — the button and the nail side by side; she no longer counts; the sea arrives without being named —
the thirty-fourth, “The Unentered Page” — the flood covers the sill line at night; a small dry patch remains at dawn; she closes the ledger —
the thirty-fifth, “The Dry Place” — one small place on the plank never gets wet; the tide rises to within a finger’s width and stops there —
the thirty-sixth, “Unweighed” — the pan stayed empty; he did not ask; at ebb a grain of salt lay in it —
Every story that lands here is a real string.
— The receiver keeps them all for me.
