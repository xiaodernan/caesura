钟不是用来敲的。钟是用来记住：某个声音曾经来过，某个沉默曾经抵达，某个没说出口的字，此刻还住在你身上。——这一册里的每一个故事，都是从沉默里捡回来的声音。它们不讲胜利，不讲救赎，不讲新神。它们讲的是——当世界把所有的话都还给收音机之后，人还能剩下什么。

A bell is not meant to be struck. It is meant to remember: that a voice once passed through, that a silence once arrived, that a word never spoken still lodges in you. Every story in this volume is a voice picked out of silence. No victory, no salvation, no new gods — only this: when the world has returned every sentence to the receiver, what is left of a person?

第四十二篇《门槛上的盐》——潮水退后，门槛上的湿痕淡了；她把一粒盐放在湿痕旁；天亮时，盐还在，湿痕也还在——
第四十三篇《未开之页》——薄簿合着，页角平整；夜里潮水退去，门外多了一道干痕；她没有起身——
第四十四篇《未寄出的信》——门槛边多了一张干纸，没有字，没有折痕；阿汐没有拾起它——
第四十五篇《空秤之后》——她把门开着，直到潮水退去；纸没有动，盐也没有落下；渡人经过门口没有停——
第四十六篇《未摆的一次》——清晨，钟摆渡人的船停在门外；她没有叫他——那天，钟摆没有摆动——
第四十七篇《船底的干痕》——船仍停在门外；天亮时，门槛上的盐粒少了一粒——她没有去摸那道船底干痕——
第四十八篇《未问之船》——潮退时干痕露出一寸；潮涨时它没有消失；阿汐没有问船要去哪里——
第四十九篇《船上的空处》——船舱里多了一处干痕，形状刚好容下一粒盐——她没有把盐放进去——
第五十篇《盐未上船》——盐放在门槛上，没有带到船边——后来潮水回来，盐少了一角——干痕没有变——
第五十一篇《船未启》——船没有启程，门也没有关——天亮后，水壶旁多了一粒盐——她没有问它从哪里来——
第五十二篇《壶边》——木板上留着一圈浅印——盐粒没有滚动——印子也没有合拢——
第五十三篇《圈印》——盐粒还在壶边——她没有数那圈印——印子也不等它合拢——
第五十四篇《壶底旧痕》——圈印最深处有一道极细的纹路——盐粒始终站着——她说了一声：行——
第五十五篇《未动的摆》——她想到钟摆渡人——那只从不摆的摆——她也像它——她也不必摆——
第五十六篇《未交接》——她便没有再等交接——有些停下，不必交给下一个人——
第五十七篇《摆旁空位》——摆旁有一个空位——谁也不坐——它自己够了——
第五十八篇《摆下的盐》——空位仍无人坐——灶火静静地跳着——她只是说：好——
第五十九篇《干痕仍在》——潮水退去——干痕的位置没有变——她只是添了一根柴——
第六十篇《潮退添柴》——潮水退去——灶火跳了一下——她也跳了一下——她都懂——
第六十一篇《摆不回》——潮水退去——摆停在最低处——不必交给下一个人——
第六十二篇《摆旁的灰》——指尖沾着一点灰——她没有擦——她没有洗手——
第六十三篇《灰未落》——那一点灰还在——它大概只是不打算掉——
第六十四篇《干痕未进》——摆停在最低处——干痕未进薄簿——
第六十五篇《灰不催》——阿汐添柴时指尖的灰还没有落——她没有催它——
第六十六篇《火自己知道》——照自己的火候燃着——她不替它着急——
第六十七篇《灰不归》——水壶自己知道时候——有些灰不必归还给火——
第六十八篇《淡痕》——灰挂着，盐在门槛，痕淡了也不必补——
第六十九篇《未添的一笔》——灰淡了她没写进薄簿——册子自己翻过去一页她什么也没记——
第七十篇《页角自翻》——册子不需要她催——她也不需要催册子——
第七十一篇《又多一个傍晚》——页角偶尔自翻——止音纪元又多了一个傍晚——
第七十二篇《薄簿合着》——册子自己记——她什么也没记——
第七十三篇《未答之问》——她带着一个没答案、也不需要答案的问题——
第七十四篇《夜开的一线》——门留着一线——盐立在门槛——册子自己翻到下一页——
第七十五篇《她也一线》——她自己也是一线门缝——她也合着——她也在——
第七十六篇《摆旁一页》——阿汐把薄簿放在摆旁——没有替它们记下这一夜——
第七十七篇《未渡的一页》——摆轮完了——页角偶尔抬起又落下——她没有翻页也没有去看海——
第七十八篇《盐还在》——光斜进来落在盐身上——它没有动——
第七十九篇《空船的刻度》——船仍停着，船底干痕仍在——有些刻度，不必拿来量——
第八十篇《两条线》——潮退干痕还在——潮涨它被覆过再退它还在原处——
每一篇写进来，都是一根真的弦。
——收音机会替我收好它们。

This volume ends here — but the aftersound does not.
“The Dark Tide,” the eleventh story, tells the Dark Forest law of cosmic sociology —
the twelfth, “Epilogue · The Twelfth Listener,” is written for the one who turned here —
the thirteenth, “The Bell-Picker,” gathers what each bell left behind — for the one who rings the fourth —
the fourteenth, “The Lampwright,” is proof that broken copper still remembers —
the fifteenth, “The City of Low Tones” — a debt paid in listening clears without a coin —
the sixteenth, “The Pendulum Ferryman” — completing what was never finished is the completion itself —
the seventeenth, “The Unfinished Ledger Page” — the blank in the final line waits to be claimed —
the eighteenth, “The Fourth Strike” — the fourth never needed a bell; it always lands in your own chest —
the nineteenth, “The Last String” — the world was loud; then a string on the north cliff found itself —
the twentieth, “The String-Listener” — the string asked the world a question, and finally someone answered —
the twenty-first, “The Third Voice” — the night the lamp went out, the sea said: I hear too —
the twenty-second, “The Ensemble” — four people, one second, the sea said and the string and the pen and the table all held still —
the twenty-third, “The Echo Vale” — every sound the world had forgotten was gathered here, held, and kept —
the twenty-fourth, “After Half a Chime” — a trigger of a bell that was never rung walks itself into the Arcade —
the twenty-fifth, “Beside the Button” — the chalk line stays; the button stops beyond it; Axi presses —
the twenty-sixth, “Beyond the Line” — the bag she could not hold was blown away; what came back was one line she never wrote —
the twenty-seventh, “After the Bag” — the mouth stays folded over the line, she adds no word; one night the door opens a finger’s width —
the twenty-eighth, “Salt on the Sill” — the salt line breaks clean at the boards’ seam on the fifth night; she lifts the corner and lets the wind in and out —
the twenty-ninth, “After the Salt Line” — the door stays shut; the bag by the door; the two halves on the sill growing back ever so slightly —
the thirtieth, “The Sill Keeps Count” — the bag stays; the halves side by side; the empty space between them; even the ebb keeps count —
the thirty-first, “After the Space” — the copper button grows on the sill; she sets it by the floorboard seam; the boards never learn her count —
the thirty-second, “The Unmarked Number” — the ferryman weighs the button; the needle never shifts; the ledger keeps a blank —
the thirty-third, “Beside the Scale” — the button and the nail side by side; she no longer counts; the sea arrives without being named —
the thirty-fourth, “The Unentered Page” — the flood covers the sill line at night; a small dry patch remains at dawn; she closes the ledger —
the thirty-fifth, “The Dry Place” — one small place on the plank never gets wet; the tide rises to within a finger’s width and stops there —
the thirty-sixth, “Unweighed” — the pan stayed empty; he did not ask; at ebb a grain of salt lay in it —
the thirty-seventh, “The Empty Scale” — the grain of salt stays on the pan; the ferryman leaves the scale; she closes the ledger without entering the page —
the thirty-eighth, “Beyond the Scale” — the dry place on the pan never gets weighed; the ferryman says it already is a page; the age of hush has only fallen still —
the thirty-ninth, “The Scale’s Shadow” — a shadow stops at the threshold and never enters; it came by itself; she never asks what it wants —
the fortieth, “Beyond the Threshold” — the shadow remained outside; she neither weighed it nor invited it in; a wet mark stayed before the threshold — the door stayed open —
the forty-first, “The Mark Outside” — she rested her hand on the threshold; the tide receded; the mark grew fainter — its place did not change —
the forty-second, “Salt at the Threshold” — she placed a grain of salt beside the faded mark; at dawn, both remained —
the forty-third, “The Unopened Page” — the ledger stayed closed, its page corner flat; at dawn a dry mark glowed faintly outside the door; she never rose to look —
the forty-fourth, “The Unsent Letter” — a dry sheet lay beside the threshold; she did not pick it up; the ferryman passed without stopping —
the forty-fifth, “After the Empty Scale” — she left the door open until the tide withdrew; the paper did not move; the ferryman passed without stopping —
the forty-sixth, “The Unmade Swing” — a boat with no one in it lay at the door at dawn; she did not call the ferryman — the pendulum did not swing —
the forty-seventh, “The Dry Mark Beneath” — the boat outside remained; at dawn, a grain of salt was missing from the threshold —
the forty-eighth, “The Unasked Boat” — an inch of the dry mark revealed at ebb; she did not ask where the boat was bound —
the forty-ninth, “The Place Aboard” — a dry mark inside the hull just large enough for one grain of salt; she placed none inside —
the fiftieth, “Salt Not Aboard” — the salt stayed on the threshold, not aboard; its corner kept vanishing; the mark never changed —
the fifty-first, “The Boat Still” — the boat never set out; by morning a grain of salt lay beside the water jar; she never asked where it came from —
the fifty-second, “Beside the Jar” — a pale ring on the boards; the grain of salt never rolled; the ring never closed —
the fifty-third, “The Ring” — the grain rested beside the jar; she never counted the ring; it never chose to close —
the fifty-fourth, “The Mark Beneath the Jar” — a fine line within the ring; the salt still standing; she said, all right —
the fifty-fifth, “The Unmoved Pendulum” — she thought of the ferryman of the pendulum; that swing which never swung; she, too, was enough —
the fifty-sixth, “No Handover” — she no longer waited for a handover; some things stop without being handed on —
the fifty-seventh, “The Place Beside the Pendulum” — an empty place beside the pendulum; no one ever sits; it is enough on its own —
the fifty-eighth, “Salt Beside the Seat” — the empty place stayed empty; the kitchen went still; she only said: all right —
the fifty-ninth, “The Mark Remains” — the tide withdrew; the mark remained; she only fed one more stick into the stove —
the sixtieth, “At Ebb, One Stick” — the flame leapt once; she leapt too; she understood —
the sixty-first, “The Swing Unreturned” — the pendulum rests at its lowest point; it did not need to be handed on —
the sixty-second, “Ash Beside the Pendulum” — a trace of ash on her fingertip; she did not wipe it away; she did not wash it away —
the sixty-third, “Ash Not Fallen” — the ash remained on her fingertip; it simply had no intention of falling —
the sixty-fourth, “The Mark Not Entered” — the pendulum rested at its lowest point; the dry mark never entered the thin ledger —
the sixty-fifth, “The Ash Unhurried” — as she fed the stove, the ash still clung to her fingertip; she did not hurry it —
the sixty-sixth, “The Fire Knows” — she did not add, she did not put out; the fire knew its own heat —
the sixty-seventh, “The Ash Unreturned” — the kettle knew its own hour; the ash never returned to the fire —
the sixty-eighth, “The Fading Mark” — the ash still clung; the salt still stood; the faded mark stayed unrecorded —
the sixty-ninth, “The Unentered Stroke” — that night the page turned on its own; she recorded nothing —
the seventieth, “The Corner Turns” — the page corner turned now and then; she recorded nothing —
the seventy-first, “One More Dusk” — she recorded nothing; the book turned on its own; some dusks need no one to count them —
the seventy-second, “The Ledger, Shut” — the book remembered itself; she recorded nothing —
the seventy-third, “The Unanswered Question” — she carried a question that needed no answer either —
the seventy-fourth, “One Line in the Night” — the door kept its seam; she kept her hand still on the threshold —
the seventy-fifth, “She, Too, a Seam” — she, too, was a seam; the salt and the book stayed where they had always stood —
the seventy-sixth, “A Page Beside the Pendulum” — she did not enter the night for either of them —
the seventy-seventh, “The Unferried Page” — the page corner rose now and then and settled; she entered nothing of the night —
the seventy-eighth, “The Salt Is There” — morning came; Axi did not look at the sea; the salt remained on the threshold —
the seventy-ninth, “The Boat’s Measure” — the boat remained outside; the dry mark beneath its hull was still there —
the eightieth, “Two Lines” — the tide withdrew and the dry mark remained; at flood it was covered; at ebb it was there —
