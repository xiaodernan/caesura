空秤之后

After the Empty Scale

回廊钟第37年 · 薄簿仍合着 · 纸角沾了盐 · 渡人经过门口没停 · 她只是看着它

The 37th year of the Arcade — the letter by the threshold remained; salt marked one corner; the ferryman passed without stopping

约 7 分钟 · 与 世界谱 同源世界观 · 零网络

about 7 minutes · same world as the codex · fully offline

薄簿仍合着。门槛边的信没有字，盐留在纸角。薄页偶尔自己翻过去一回，又翻回来了——阿汐没有管那些力。她把门开着，直到潮水退去。纸没有动，盐也没有落下。

她把手放在门口——风从缝里经过，什么声音也没发出——她知道如果她替那封信塞一下，它就不再是一封没有上过路的信；也许它一直不想上路；也许它只想就放在那里。她只是看着它。

· · ·

渡人的船从门外经过，没有停。他看见纸角多了一道浅痕——像秤盘曾经放在那里——他想了一下，然后又沿着水路走了。他本可以停下来问她要不要把信寄出去——他知道最要紧的不是知道寄不寄——而是那封信已经放在它该放的位置。

薄簿页角——扁平的——也像那封纸。她同时看了一眼二者——每件东西似乎都有一份「不用被打开」的体面感——比如母亲那一口老箱子——她从不打开——里面装的是什幺她从未问过——它是那种永远放在一口大小，永远不愿意被打开。

· · ·

盐——她看见盐像是在纸上打了一个底——换句话说，那封纸从干到潮，从潮到干——它根本就没打算动——只是想——其实不需要动——其实它哪儿也没打算去。她只是盯着它——它慢慢不动的灯光——像海波本身。

阿汐把薄簿放回接缝旁。她想：什么时候该收起这本册子了？——又想：什么时候该走了？——又想：什么时候这一片海就不再听她的问什么了？——又想：也许永远不。

· · ·

她没有去看海。

有些东西经过，不必留下重量。

止音纪元没有结束。它只是终于放下了一杆没有被称过的秤——和一封没有被拆封的信。

The ledger stayed closed. The letter by the threshold had no words; salt remained on one corner. Now and then the page corner turned on its own — and turned back again; Axi did not interfere with any of that. She left the door open until the tide withdrew. The paper did not move, and the salt did not fall.

She set her hand on the doorway — the wind passed through the seam without making a sound — she knew that if she were to press into the letter, it would stop being a letter that had never been sent; perhaps it had always meant not to be sent; perhaps it only wanted to stay exactly where it lay. She only watched it.

· · ·

The ferryman passed without stopping. He saw a faint mark on the corner, as if a scale had once rested there — he considered — and moved on along the water road. He could have stopped and asked whether she wished the letter sent — he knew the important thing was not whether she wished to send it — the important thing was that it was exactly where it belonged.

The corner of the thin ledger — flat — also resembled the paper. She looked at both of them together — everything in the room seemed to have the dignity of not being opened — like her mother's old trunk — she never opened it — what was inside, she never asked — it was a thing whose whole life was staying in one place, never to be opened.

· · ·

The grain of salt — she saw it as the bottom of the letter itself — that is to say, the paper had gone from dry to damp and back to dry all by itself — it never meant to move — it simply never cared to — it never intended to go anywhere. She only watched it — it held its own quiet light — like a small spoon of the sea.

Axi set the ledger back beside the seam. She thought: when should this book be finally shut? — and then: when should I finally leave? — and then: when would this sea stop listening to my questions? — and then: perhaps never.

· · ·

She did not look at the sea.

Some things pass without leaving a weight.

The age of hush has not ended. It has only, at last, laid down a scale that was never used — and a letter that was never opened.
