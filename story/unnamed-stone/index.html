Short story · Resthold · No. 2 “The Nameless Stone”

山下无名碑

The Nameless Stone

休垒 · 山脚 · 石匠的女儿阿汀

Resthold · at the foot of the hill · A Ting, the stonecutter’s daughter

约 5 分钟 · 世界谱 与 游戏 同源世界观 · 零 LLM 撰写

about 5 minutes · same world as the codex and the game · written without LLM assistance

休垒山下的那块石碑，没有名字。

每天傍晚，石匠的女儿阿汀要沿山脚小路走一趟——拂一遍灰、添一勺水，再说一句无关紧要的话。这是她父亲临终前交给她的唯一一项活计。

· · ·

第一年，她问父亲：这是给谁的？

父亲说：给被遗忘的人。

第二年，她问：你叫得出那个人的名字吗？

父亲说：他没有名字。

第三年，她开始自己回答：这是给第一口钟的——钟响了又停了，没有人替它收尾。

· · ·

休垒人不信这个。他们说这碑是给那些哑了的人——哑了的人死透了，却不知道自己正在被记得。休垒人管这叫「最后的照面」。

阿汀没有反驳。她只是每次拂碑时，悄悄选一个位置，用指节轻轻磕三下——像父亲敲过的那种「锁紧」。

· · ·

后来，休垒的钟终于响了。

没有人看得见是谁敲的——人人都在自己的屋里，听那一记。阿汀走到山下，把手掌平贴在石碑上，等。

等到了。

· · ·

碑里传来一声极低极长的共振——不是钟声，而是钟声的注脚，像什么东西替无名的石碑签了一个名。

石碑没有碎，也没有亮。它只是不再是一块无名的石头了。

它变成了休垒唯一一块知道自己被记得的石头。

· · ·

她收回手，对着碑说了一句从没说过的话：

“这算两个人了。你，和替你响的那一记。”

年复一年，休垒人问阿汀那碑是给谁的，她都答不上来。直到有一年，她给了一个答案：

“给不知道自己还能被记得的人。”

说完这句，她照例朝石碑轻轻地点了一下头——就像父亲敲过一千次的「锁紧」。

The stone under the hill has no name.

Every evening, A Ting, the stonecutter’s daughter, walks the path beneath the hill — a pass with a cloth, a bowl of water, a sentence of no consequence. It is the only work her father left her before he died.

· · ·

The first year, she asked him: whose is it for?

He said: for the forgotten.

The second year, she asked: can you say the person’s name?

He said: there is no name.

The third year, she began to answer herself: it is for the First Bell — it rang and then it stopped, and no one closed it out.

· · ·

The people of Resthold did not believe her. They said the stone was for those who went mute — for those who died mute, unaware that they were being remembered even as they were being forgotten. They called it “the last crossing.”

A Ting did not argue. She only, each time, found a spot on the stone and knocked it three times with her knuckles — a “lock-tight” her father had knocked a thousand times.

· · ·

Then, one winter, the bell of Resthold rang.

No one saw who rang it — everyone was indoors, listening. A Ting walked down the hill, laid her flat palm against the stone, and waited.

It came.

· · ·

From inside the stone came a very low, very long resonance — not the bell itself, but the bell’s footnote, as if something were signing for the stone at last.

The stone did not crack, and it did not shine. It simply was no longer nameless.

It had become the only stone in Resthold that knew it was remembered.

· · ·

She drew her hand back and said to the stone a sentence she had never said:

“That makes two of us. You, and the one who rang on your behalf.”

Year after year, when people asked A Ting whose stone it was, she could not answer — until one year she finally gave one:

“For those who don’t know they can be remembered.”

Then, as ever, she gave the stone one small nod — the same small nod she had seen her father give a thousand times, the one he called his lock-tight.
