Short story · Resthold · No. 4 “The Ledger”

账本

The Ledger

休垒 · 虚礼典礼官 Meg 的主账房

Resthold · Meg the Valet-Master-of-the-Ledger's counting house

约 5 分钟 · 世界谱 与 游戏 同源世界观 · 零 LLM 撰写

about 5 minutes · same world as the codex and the game · written without LLM assistance

麦格的主账房在休垒的旧街区末尾，靠近水缸，靠近大家避讳的那口钟。

· · ·

账上的字，她记得最清楚的一笔是四十七天前——一个面色异常、但腰上的金很旧的人，买了一张新弦、一块音色石，用现银付了，但在"余额"一栏里她没写数字。

她只写了一个字：未。

· · ·

她从小就学会一件事：有些事如果急着结清，就会永远消失。所以账房里她养了三本账——官账、街账，和一本她自己的，谁都没看过。

第三本账的最后一页，只有一行是空的。

· · ·

她翻了很多年，终于看懂那一行写的是什么：『这笔账不能算。对方不是登记的人。』

她记不清那是哪一年、哪一个人。但她记得那个人来的时候，手里捏着一块还没被敲过的钟铜——她收了那块铜，却从来没记过这笔账，仿佛他从来不曾走进过这个门。

· · ·

“原来不是他没付。是他不允许我收。”

他不是来买东西的。他是来把那块钟铜还回去的——还给世界里一个已经不认识它的人。

· · ·

她最后一次翻那本账的时候，在缺了一行的地方补上了一笔她从不敢写的账：

“已收。”

“那个不可以收的人，终于把不可以收的东西，收回去了。”

她把第三本账放回架子最高处。那天以后，她再没数过任何一页。

Meg’s counting house sits at the back of Resthold’s old market, near the water jars, near the bell everyone avoids.

· · ·

Among all the entries, the one she remembered best was forty-seven days ago: a man with an unusually fair face but very old gold at his belt, who bought a new cord and a resonance stone, paid in coin — and in the “balance” column she wrote no number.

She only wrote one word: outstanding.

· · ·

Since childhood she had been taught one rule: some things, if you rush to settle them, vanish forever. So in her counting house she kept three ledgers — an official ledger, a street ledger, and one of her own that no one else ever saw.

The third ledger, on its last page, had only one line that was empty.

· · ·

She spent years turning its pages before she could finally read the line: “This entry cannot stand. The other party is not who I think.”

She no longer remembered what year, or which person. But she remembered him coming in, carrying a piece of bell metal that had never been struck — she accepted it, and she never entered this transaction in any book. As if he had never passed through the door.

· · ·

“He never owed me. He never meant for me to take it.”

He was not buying anything. He was returning the bell metal — to a world that no longer knew how to hold it.

· · ·

The last time she opened that ledger, she filled the missing line with an entry she had never once allowed herself to write:

“Received.”

“The one person who could never receive anything, finally received what he had come to return.”

She put the third ledger back on the top shelf. From that day on, she never counted another page.
