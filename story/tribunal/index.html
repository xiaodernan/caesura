雨审判

The Rain Tribunal

余响新陆 · 雨钟谷地 · 雨罪审判者

The Afterland · the Rainbell Valley · The Drowning Jurist

约 5 分钟 · 与 世界谱 同源世界观 · 设定册《新六地域》同源 · 零 LLM 撰写

about 5 minutes · same world as the codex · from the New Six Lands design canon · written without LLM assistance

雨钟谷地的审判，每七年开一次庭。没有原告，没有被告席——法庭上只有一位审判者，和一位把所有东西都记在纸上的书记。

· · ·

雨罪审判者穿一件从不干的法袍。它的袖口从不滴水，但掌心永远是湿的——那是雨住在布里面，谁也不知道它从什么时候开始的。

他的起诉书上写着七条沉钟罪责：七口钟在雨里各自犯过什么。纸上全是被泡散又凝固的字，没有人看得清——不是不想看，是这份纸，从落笔那天起，就没有干过。

· · ·

雨书记坐在法庭的角落，负责记。他的字一次次被雨水泡散又重新写深——他从第一个字学会的唯一一件事，就是在雨还没来得及泡掉它之前，先把它压进纸里。

七年又七年，他的纸堆了七摞。每一摞，都等同一件事：等一个人，愿意说一句——不管是认罪，还是辩护。

· · ·

这一场，念到的是第七条罪责：「谷地自己的钟，未经允许，替它自己自愿转身。」

审判者念了整整一个钟头。雨声替他念了大半句——句号之间，所有的钟声都在雨里学着一起落。这一条究竟有没有犯过，七年一审、审了七年，答案始终一样：有记录的，那是谷地自愿的。

· · ·

就在第七年的法庭里，雨书记突然站了起来。

审判者看着他。七场散会的光线都是这样灰的——没有一个记录会被收回，因为没人想加进这条被纸泡掉的东西里。

「书记，请看见。」

· · ·

不是辩护，不是认罪，不是证词——法庭里所有的词语前面，从来没人说过「请看见」。

审判者停了。雨里，谷地那口沉钟的钟壳里，滚落出自己也说不清楚的那一下——不是认罪，也不是辩护，而是「在」。

· · ·

审判者把起诉书合上。七条罪责里，没有一条从此消失——但审判也不再只是审判。

雨书记这一次没有低头重抄。这一页，留了下来。字迹还在——因为它压进纸里的那一笔，是他写字以来，第一笔没被雨泡掉的字。

雨没有停。法庭空了七年的那个空位，终于有人坐了下来。

The Rainbell Valley sits in session once every seven years. There is no prosecutor, no dock — there is one jurist, and one clerk who writes everything down.

· · ·

The Drowning Jurist wears a robe that never dries. Its cuffs never drip, but its palms are forever water — the rain lives inside the cloth, and no one remembers when it started.

His indictment lists seven charges — seven bells, and what each one did in the rain. The writing is nothing but dissolved and re-hardened strokes; nobody can read it. Not because anyone refuses — but no page written in that room has once been dry.

· · ·

The clerk sits in the corner, keeping the record. Every line he writes is drowned, then written deeper — the only lesson he ever learned was to press the words into the page before the water could carry them off.

Seven years upon seven years, and he leaves seven stacks of paper. Every stack stands for the same thing — waiting for one person, willing to say one sentence: an admission, or a defense.

· · ·

This time, he reads charge seven: “The valley’s own bell, with no one’s permission, turned itself away.”

The jurist reads for a full hour. The rain itself reads most of it — between every period, all the bells learn to fall in learning how to fall. Whether the charge stands, seven years upon seven years of verdict has always come to one thing: the valley turned, and that is on record.

· · ·

And in that same seventh year, the clerk stands up.

The jurist looks at him. The light has been this grey for seven sessions — nobody recorded anything, since nobody ever wanted to add to what the paper had already carried away.

“Clerk. Please see.”

· · ·

Not a defense. Not an admission. Not a testimony — not one word in this room had ever before been preceded by please see.

The jurist stops. And from the shell of the sunken bell, a single tone it could not explain rolled through the rain — not an admission, not a defense, only is.

· · ·

The jurist closes the indictment. None of the seven charges vanish — but the court is no longer only a court.

The clerk does not copy the page again. This one stays. The writing is still there — because that one stroke was the first in his life the rain could not dissolve.

The rain never stopped. And the empty seat that had stood empty for seven years — finally, someone sat down.
