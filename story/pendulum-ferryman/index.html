Short story · The Aftermath · No. 16

钟摆渡人

The Pendulum Ferryman

替时间摆、给从不会来的人摆

Swinging for time itself, for the one who never comes

约 7 分钟 · 与 世界谱 同源世界观 · 零网络

about 7 minutes · same world as the codex · fully offline

在未竟厅最深处，有一座钟摆——它不是给钟用的，是给时间用的。它从老大师造完那座从没敲响的钟之后就开始摆，摆了三十年。

钟摆渡人就是那座钟摆的守摆人——每天早上她要把摆从最高一边推到最低一边，再从最低一边推到最高一边。一天推一百零二次，正好对上那座从没被敲响的钟里压电的速率。

· · ·

她年轻时曾经想过：为什么是她？她母亲在井边死的时候，老大师把这句问了她。她没有回答——她只是看着那口从没敲响的钟，它还在等那个从不会来的人。她忽然明白了：那个答案不重要。

她搬进了未竟厅，一住就是三十年。她学会了什么时候推、什么时候不推——摆自己有自己的节律；她学会了如何绕过那些「欠着的债」——每张谱子最后一页都被她按着不翻过去，因为她不介意自己替它们的主人再等一遍。

· · ·

走进未竟厅的人不知道：钟摆的每次摆动，都会替厅里某个人放下一样东西。一个老兵把一枚从未发出去过的命令放在钟摆最低点，离开了。一个母亲把一句从没说出口的告别放在某一天里，再也没有回来。一个孩子放了一颗螺丝钉——她看了看，说：「你以后再来补它。」

· · ·

多年后，恩希尔忽然要离开未竟厅。她知道那就是「等她太久」的那一刻。她没有试图留他——钟摆摆到最低点时她轻轻推了一下，摆就荡起来了。

她在想：他走了之后，谁还会替她来看这面摆？

· · ·

如今未竟厅最深处仍然有一个钟摆。只是它旁边多了一把椅子——守摆人现在会偶尔坐在那里喝口茶，然后再站起来。

每年只有一次，她会把摆从最高一边推到最低一边，然后站在最低点不放手——不推它，等它自己慢慢停下来。摆停下来的那一刻，未竟厅就算真正被完成了。

完成一件从没被完成的事——
这件事本身就是完成。

At the deepest reach of the Unfinished Hall stands a pendulum. It is not for a bell; it is for time. It began swinging the moment the Last Master finished crafting the bell that was never struck, and it has swung for thirty years since.

The Pendulum Ferryman is the pendulum's keeper — every morning she pushes the pendulum from its highest edge to its lowest, then from the lowest back to the highest. One hundred and two pushes a day, tuned exactly to the tick rate locked inside the never-struck bell.

· · ·

When she was young, she used to wonder: why her? When her mother died by the well, the Last Master put that question to her. She did not answer — she simply looked at the never-struck bell, still waiting for the one who would never come. At that moment she understood: the answer did not matter.

She moved into the hall and stayed for thirty years. She learned when to push, when not to — the pendulum kept its own rhythm; she learned how to step around the debts that were owed — every score's last page remained under her hand, never turned, because she didn't mind waiting once more on behalf of its owner.

· · ·

Everyone who walked into the Unfinished Hall never knew: every swing of the pendulum set down something at the hall for somebody. An old soldier placed an order-command that was never issued at the pendulum's lowest point, and left. A mother left one unspoken farewell on one certain day, and never returned. A little boy left a screw — the Ferryman looked at it and said: “you can come back to fit it in later.”

· · ·

Years later, Hersil finally announced he was leaving the Unfinished Hall. She knew that was the moment he had been waiting for. She did not try to stop him — at the lowest point she tapped the pendulum once, and it swung away.

She wondered: who would come to look in on this pendulum after he was gone?

· · ·

Even today, at the deepest reach of the Unfinished Hall, the pendulum still swings. Only now there is a chair beside it — the Ferryman occasionally sits there with a cup of tea, then stands again.

Once a year, she pushes the pendulum once from its highest edge to its lowest and then keeps her hand on the lowest point — she does not push it, letting it come gradually to a stop. The moment the pendulum comes to rest, the Unfinished Hall is complete.

Completing something that was never finished —
itself is the completion.
