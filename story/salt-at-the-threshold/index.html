门槛上的盐

Salt at the Threshold

回廊钟第37年 · 她把一粒盐放在湿痕旁 · 湿痕淡了 · 她没有记下盐 · 天亮时盐还在

The 37th year of the Arcade — she placed a grain of salt beside the faded mark; she never entered it; at dawn, both remained

约 7 分钟 · 与 世界谱 同源世界观 · 零 LLM 撰写

about 7 minutes · same world as the codex · written without LLM assistance

潮水退后，门槛上的湿痕淡了。阿汐没有擦。薄簿仍合着，页角没有翻动。她从袖里掏出一粒盐——不知是怎么把它藏下来的，也不想为什么——她把它放在湿痕旁边，没有记下它。

天亮时，盐还在，湿痕也还在。木板被晒得很浅，湿痕呈现出一种很淡的颜色，比潮湿的木板浅一点、比干的也深一点。盐粒看着潮湿的光线，自己也像发光的——它就在那里。

· · ·

渡人某天又停在门口。阿汐没有叫他进来，她也没有出去。她只是隔着门槛看看他——他好像正在数着盐粒笑——他也有一粒盐藏在自己袖子里。他也没有把那粒盐拿出来。

她想起阿汐小时候母亲曾给过她一粒盐，说：盐会等你回来——它比你以为的耐心长很多。阿汐以为它说的是等饭，或者等池塘里的鱼。她没有真正听懂。也许她今天才懂了一点。

渡人说：它跟以前是一回事——还是不一样的。他说完就走了，走之前也没有回头。他的背影在她的眼里像一页薄簿慢慢翻走。

· · ·

阿汐又把薄簿拿起来看了一眼。薄页仍一动不动——它仍然是那一页没写下的那一页。她把它放回接缝旁。她慢慢明白：世界的某一页它就是这这样一页——它一直都在等的不是被记住，而是被放着。

她没有再拿起它。

· · ·

她没有看海。

有些留下，不必登记。

止音纪元没有结束。它只是终于留下了一粒盐——盐不知道它被留下的那天起就开始发光了。

After the tide withdrew, the mark on the threshold grew faint. Axi did not wipe it away. The ledger stayed closed, its page corner still. She took a grain of salt from her sleeve — she had no idea how it had gotten there, nor why she held it — and placed it beside the mark, and did not enter it.

At dawn, both remained. The boards were bleached pale; the mark showed in a faint shade — paler than damp wood, darker than any dry board. The grain of salt seemed to catch the new light and glow a little on its own — it was just there.

· · ·

One day the ferryman happened by. Axi did not call him in, nor did she come out. She only watched him over the threshold — he seemed to be smiling, as if there were a grain of salt of his own hidden away in his sleeve. He never brought it out either.

She remembered that, as a girl, her mother had once handed her a grain of salt, saying: salt will wait for you — it is far more patient than you think. Axi had assumed she was talking about rice, or a fish in a pond. She had never really understood. Perhaps, today, she had begun to understand a little.

The ferryman said: it is the same as before — or not the same. He left after that, without looking back. His back receded from her eyes like a thin page of the ledger turning on its own.

· · ·

Axi lifted the ledger once more and looked at it. The page did not move — it was still the page that had never been written. She set it down beside the seam. She came slowly to know: there is a rule this world runs by — that page is not waiting to be remembered; it is waiting to be held.

She did not pick it up again.

· · ·

She did not look at the sea.

Some things remain without being entered.

The age of hush has not ended. It has only, at last, left behind one grain of salt — it began glowing the day it was left.
