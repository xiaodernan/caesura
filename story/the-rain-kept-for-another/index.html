替人记的雨

The Rain Kept for Another

雨审判 · 书记员的簿子 · 替不识字的人记下的三十七天

The Rain Tribunal — a clerk's ledger, thirty-seven days kept for a man who could not read

约 6 分钟 · 与 世界谱 同源世界观 · 零网络

about 6 minutes · same world as the codex · fully offline

雨审判那天，庭上一句话也没有。

阿汐那天也在庭上，她记得雨声。

书记员坐在窗边，把雨一笔一笔记下来：落了几刻，什么时候大，什么时候小，风从哪一边来。

一个老农站在最后，说他不识字，请书记员替他记。

· · ·

书记员问：你记它做什么？

老农说：我儿子在海那边。他要是问起这里的雨，我总得有个东西给他看。

书记员说：你活不到他回来。

老农说：那就让纸活到。

他记雨不用字，用点。雨大，墨点就浓；雨小，点就淡得几乎看不见。纸被窗风吹得起了皱，他拿镇纸压住，一边压一边记。

老农每天散庭前都来问一句：今天记了吗？书记员说记了，他就点点头走，像刚领过一份口粮。

第三十七天没有雨。书记员没有写「无雨」，那一格他留了白，只在旁边写：今日无声。

因为雨停也是一种雨，得有人替它记着。

· · ·

于是他记了三十七天。第一天雨大，第十一天只有一阵，第二十二天整夜没停。第三十七天，雨停了。

那天老农没有再来。书记员去他住的地方，看见门开着，灶是冷的。

他回来，在簿子上老农那一栏下面按了一个指印——老农说过的，不会写字，只能按一下。

· · ·

后来有人翻到那一页。雨还在，指印也在。庭上早就没有雨审判了，纸倒还在下。

书记员已经不在。新来的人不懂那一栏是什么意思，就照着抄了一份，寄到海那边。

有些账不是算给活人看的。

它是为让一件发生过的事，在另一个人手里还能继续发生——哪怕记账的人和要账的人都已不在。

止音纪元没有结束。它又多了一个证据：雨停了三十七年，簿子还在替两个人下雨。

On the day of the Rain Tribunal no one in the court spoke.

Axi was in the court that day; she remembers the sound of rain.

The clerk sat by the window and wrote the rain down: how long it fell, when it grew, when it thinned, which side the wind came from.

An old farmer stood at the back. He could not read; he asked the clerk to keep the rain for him.

· · ·

The clerk asked: why write it down?

The farmer said: my son is across the sea. If he asks about the rain here, I must have something to show him.

The clerk said: you will not live till he returns.

The farmer said: then let the paper live.

He did not write the rain in words; he wrote it in dots. Heavy rain, a dark dot; light rain, a dot so pale it was almost invisible. When the window wind wrinkled the paper he held it flat with a paperweight and kept writing.

Each day before court ended the farmer came and asked: did you keep it today? The clerk said yes, and the farmer nodded and went, as if he had drawn his daily ration.

On the thirty-seventh day there was no rain. The clerk did not write "no rain". He left the column blank and wrote beside it: today, silence.

For the stopping of rain is also a kind of rain, and someone must keep it.

· · ·

So he wrote thirty-seven days. Heavy on the first; a brief shower on the eleventh; all night on the twenty-second. On the thirty-seventh the rain stopped.

The farmer did not come that day. The clerk went to where he lived; the door stood open and the stove was cold.

He came back and pressed a fingerprint beneath the farmer's column — the farmer had said it: he could not write; he could only press.

· · ·

Years later someone turned to that page. The rain was still there, and so was the print. There was no Rain Tribunal any more, but the paper was still falling.

The clerk was gone too. The newcomer did not know what the column meant, so he copied it and sent the copy across the sea.

Some ledgers are not kept for the living.

They are kept so that something which happened can keep happening in another person's hands — even when both the keeper and the one who asked are gone.

The age of hush has not ended. It has one more piece of evidence: the rain stopped thirty-seven years ago, and the ledger is still raining for two people.
